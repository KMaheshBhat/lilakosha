# 2026-08-14 fortnight update: a new source, new refinements, and unified reporting

Two weeks ago the post described the CDM gaining a unified content model (`ContentVariant`), the pipeline adopting source-explicit naming, and Gutenberg #62 entering the corpus as a first-class document.  That baseline held.  What has changed since then is the breadth of what the pipeline now ingests, refines, and reports on.

New capabilities arrived in sequence: language detection and theme resolution across the entire corpus, a second asynchronous-session source (RPGnet forum posts), an HTML-to-Markdown refiner to normalize that source's content, and a unified reporting step that eliminates four separate report modules.  A smaller but important thread of work - targeted scalpel steps, configurable language targeting, and source-aware health breakdown - threaded through the same period.

The corpus now stands at 26,295 records (16,833 PIPPA + 1 Gutenberg + 9,461 RPGnet threads).

## Language detection: what the corpus is saying

The first new refinement subsystem was language identification.  The corpus contains prose from multiple sources written in different registers, and there was no reliable way to ask "how many records are primarily French?" or "which documents have mixed-language turns?".

The `refine-cdm-language` step uses `lingua-language-detector` to perform windowed language identification across turn and narrative items.  It writes a `lilakosha:g1:language` `CategorizationItem` onto each record, and the corresponding `scalpel-cdm-language` step can surgically remove those categorizations when they need to be regenerated.  The `calculate_health` and `calculate_stats` routines in `cdm/meta.py` were updated to surface language distributions alongside the existing health and stats output.

A companion migration step (`migrate-pippa`) ran first to rename legacy PIPPA annotation kinds into the new `refine-pippa-*` namespace, keeping the annotation model consistent before language categorizations were layered on top.

Two follow-up refinements arrived shortly after:

- **Configurable language targets.**  The original implementation hardcoded a scan over turn items.  The `cdm_language_target` parameter now lets pipeline operators select which CDM item kinds to inspect (`narrative`, `turn`, `world`, `character`, `summary`) and which content variant to prefer for text extraction, with an ordered fallback chain.  This makes the step usable for sources where the prose lives on a different item kind than PIPPA conversations.
- **Source-aware health percentages.**  The `report-record-health` step was corrected to compute stage breakdown percentages against the correct source denominator - e.g. language-refinement pass rates are calculated against PIPPA records, not the entire corpus.  This prevents a small source from distorting corpus-wide percentages.

## Theme resolution: from tags to clusters

The second new pipeline subsystem addresses corpus-level semantic structure.  The existing theme tags on individual records are a useful signal, but they are free-text labels drawn from source-specific vocabularies.  There was no mechanism to discover which tags mean the same thing across the corpus.

The `resolve-theme-proposal` step normalizes raw theme tags (converting hyphens and underscores to spaces), embeds them with `bge-small-en-v1.5`, and runs agglomerative clustering (cosine distance, threshold=0.35) to produce a machine-readable `theme_clusters.json` manifest and a human-curatable `theme_clusters.csv` worklist.  The companion `resolve-theme-curation` step reads the curated CSV, applies drop/keep actions, and upserts a `lilakosha:g1:theme` `CategorizationItem` on every CDM record.

The old extract/cluster steps were removed and replaced with this two-phase workflow.  The pipeline entry point was renamed to `40-resolve-themes-proposal.yml` to reflect the new structure.  This is the first pass at corpus-level semantic organization - the clustering threshold and embedding model are opinionated starting points, and the curation step exists so those opinions can be corrected by inspection.

## Acquisition: a unified raw landing zone

Both the theme resolution pipeline and the RPGnet ingestion step depend on a reliable local cache of source data.  The acquisition layer was restructured around a `raw` volume that serves as a unified landing zone for all non-PIPPA source material.

`acquire_gutenberg_62()` was redirected to write into this landing zone instead of fetching over the network at ingestion time.  A new `acquire_pippa()` function was added to download the PygmalionAI/PIPPA dataset via `hf_hub_download`, and `acquire_rpgnet()` was added to fetch the RPGnet Parquet files from the `lemonilia/roleplaying-forums-raw` dataset on Hugging Face Hub.  All acquisition functions were refactored to accept a config dict rather than positional `Path` arguments, making the orchestration in `15-acquire.yml` straightforward.

The `embedding model acquisition` path was also refactored into the same config-driven shape, and the `snapshot_download` call was removed in favor of targeted `hf_hub_download` calls that are easier to reason about and resume.

## RPGnet: the second asynchronous-session source

The most significant structural addition is the RPGnet forum ingestion step.  The corpus now contains its first non-PIPPA asynchronous-session source: play-by-post RPG forum threads from RPGnet's Roleplay-By-Post Play Forum.

### Source shape

Before writing ingestion code, the source was characterized in detail.  The `lemonilia/roleplaying-forums-raw` dataset contains 9,463 Parquet rows across two files (~654 MB), each row holding a full XenForo 2.x HTML page.  Forum listing pages contain thread summaries; thread pages contain posts.  A single row may contain one post or many hundreds.  Thread IDs, post IDs, author IDs, and ISO-8601 timestamps are all present in the HTML markup.  A full-corpus exact-duplicate scan returned zero duplicates.  Thread tags are present in XenForo's `tagList` structure.

### Ingestion model

The ingestion step (`ingest-lemonilia-rpgnet`) parses each Parquet row, extracts individual posts from the XenForo HTML, and reconciles them against the CDM through `LedgerIndex`:

```
Parquet row → parse posts → construct Thread → Posts → locate existing Document → reconcile by post.id → persist Document → next row
```

The mapping is: `Thread → Document`, `Post → NarrativeItem`.  Thread identity is `rpgnet:thread:<thread_id>`, post identity is `rpgnet:post:<post_id>`.  Reconciliation is additive with timestamp-based replacement: a post seen before with an older timestamp is replaced by the newer representation; new posts are appended.  Posts present in the existing document but absent from the current source row are retained.

OOC material is preserved conservatively as `reasoning` on the `original` `ContentVariant` rather than discarded.  The HTML content is stored as the source representation; the HTML-to-Markdown transformation is deferred to a subsequent refinement step so that conversion rules can be iterated on without re-running ingestion.

### Design decisions

The source shape report and ingestion design documented several explicit decisions: error and non-forum pages are skipped rather than recovered; listing pages are not ingested as CDM entities; edit history is not preserved as separate content objects; thread metadata (slug, URL, tags) is descriptive rather than identity-bearing - absence of tags in a source row does not remove previously retained tags.  The design is intentionally RPGnet/XenForo-specific; no generic forum abstraction was introduced.

The production step does not write diagnostic HTML files.  Those were used during parser development and are not part of the ingestion contract.

## HTML-to-Markdown: normalizing forum prose

RPGnet posts arrive as XenForo-rendered HTML.  The `refine-cdm-html-to-markdown` step performs deterministic HTML-to-Markdown conversion using `markdownify`, operating on the `original` content variant of narrative, turn, world, character, and summary items.  This step is a refiner rather than part of ingestion so that conversion rules can be adjusted and re-run against already-ingested records without re-parsing the Parquet source.

Known constructs that required explicit treatment include `bbWrapper` content containers, headings, paragraphs and line breaks, links, images, quote blocks with attribution, spoiler constructs, code/preformatted content, HTML entities, and rendered BBCode.

A targeted `scalpel-cdm-html-to-markdown` step was added alongside the refiner to surgically remove only the `refine-cdm-html-to-markdown` `ContentVariant` from all content-carrying items, preserving the `original` variant and any co-existing refinements.  The existing `scalpel-pippa-grammar` step was narrowed in parallel to remove only the `refine-pippa-grammar` variant from `TurnItem.content` rather than stripping every non-original variant, so that co-existing refinements (e.g. `refine-pippa-characters`) are retained.

## Unified reporting: one step, five reporters

Four separate report steps had accumulated over time: `report-cdm-items`, `report-char-names`, `report-record-health`, and `report-record-stats`.  Each opened CDM records, iterated them, ran Pydantic validation, and produced its own output.  The shared record-iteration and validation logic was copy-pasted across all four.

The `report-cdm` step consolidates all four into a single module with five reporter classes (health, stats, items, char_names, turn_distribution) behind a configurable `report_sections` list.  Legacy boolean flags are retained as a fallback for backward compatibility.  An additional `report_narrative_distribution` sub-section was added inside the stats reporter, rendering narrative item count distribution immediately before the conversation turn distribution.

The `report-cdm-records` step was intentionally left in place because it operates on filesystem metadata and `LedgerIndex` state rather than opening CDM records - it was always out of scope for consolidation.

Pipeline files were migrated to use the unified step with `report_sections` lists.  A legacy test pipeline was intentionally left untouched for comparison.

## What the system did, and what it still doesn't

The system handled three significant structural changes in this period - a new refinement subsystem, a new source type, and a reporting consolidation - without requiring recovery from corruption or pipeline failure.  The RPGnet ingestion was validated against a 50-row probe producing 1,702 post records before the full 9,463-row corpus was processed.  The full ingestion produced 26,295 corpus records with 0 exact-duplicate conflicts.

The HTML-to-Markdown conversion has been applied to the RPGnet corpus but the conversion rules are still based on general `markdownify` behavior rather than iterative inspection of actual RPGnet content.  The deferral to a refinement step was intentional - it allows the rules to be tuned without re-running ingestion - but the tuning itself has not yet happened at the depth the design anticipated.

Theme clustering has been implemented and a curation worklist generated, but the curation step has not yet been run against the full corpus.  The proposal manifest exists; the canonical theme labels have not been committed.

Language detection has been applied to the full corpus and appears in health and stats reports and has been validated by manual inspection of a representative sample.

Some things remain on the shelf: corpus-wide semantic evaluation against the new theme taxonomy, judge models, rating systems, and the Gutenberg refinement path that was identified as next work in the previous post.  None of them block Generation 1.

In summary, this fortnight the corpus nearly doubled in size through the addition of an RPGnet forum source, the CDM gained a language detection and theme resolution subsystem, the acquisition layer consolidated around a unified raw landing zone, and the reporting layer reduced from four independent steps to one.  The system broke in ordinary ways - a naming slip, a scalpel scope that needed narrowing, a health-breakdown denominator that was computing against the wrong source - and increasingly I knew what to do about them.

There is still plenty to do, but the machinery for acquiring, refining, and reporting on a multi-source corpus is no longer experimental.
